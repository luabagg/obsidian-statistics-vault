# Infinite Series

## Summary

An infinite series is the sum of the terms of a sequence. The series converges when its sequence of partial sums approaches a finite limit; otherwise it diverges. Tests decide convergence; power and Taylor series represent functions.

## Prerequisites

[[Sequences]], [[Limits]]

## Learning Order

1. [[Sequences]] and [[Series Sums by Partial Sums]]
2. Special series: [[Geometric Series]], [[Telescoping Series]], [[Harmonic Series]], [[P Series]]
3. Tests: [[Comparison Tests]], [[Integral Test]], [[Ratio Test]], [[Root Test]], [[Alternating Series]], [[Absolute Convergence]]
4. Remainders: [[Estimating Series Sums]]
5. Function series: [[Power Series]], [[Taylor Series]], [[Maclaurin Series]], [[Binomial Series]]

## Definition

$$
\sum_{n=1}^\infty a_n = a_1+a_2+a_3+\cdots
$$

converges to $S$ if $S_n=\sum_{k=1}^n a_k\to S$ as $n\to\infty$.

## Common Mistakes

- Confusing sequences with series.
- Applying a test outside its hypotheses (e.g. alternating test without eventual monotonicity).
