# Calculus

## Summary

Calculus studies instantaneous change (derivatives) and accumulation (integrals), and how they are related. Multivariable calculus and infinite series extend the same toolkit to higher dimensions and function representation.

## Prerequisites

Prerequisites: algebra, functions, trigonometry, and basic analytic geometry. Helpful vault links when present: [[Analytic Geometry]].

## Learning Order

1. [[Limits]] — limits and continuity (including [[Fundamental Limits of Calculus]], [[Squeeze Theorem]])
2. [[Derivatives]] — rates of change, chain rule, implicit differentiation
3. [[Fundamental Theorem of Calculus]] — link between derivatives and integrals
4. [[Integrals]] — Riemann sums, techniques, multiple integrals
5. [[Multivariable Functions]] — partial derivatives, extrema, coordinates
6. [[Infinite Series]] — sequences, tests, power series, Taylor expansions

## Core Ideas

### Differential calculus

The derivative measures instantaneous rate of change. If $f(t)=t^2$, then $f'(t)=2t$.

### Integral calculus

The definite integral measures net accumulation. For example, $\int_0^1 x^2\,dx=1/3$.

### Fundamental theorem

[[Fundamental Theorem of Calculus]] connects differentiation and integration under standard continuity hypotheses.
